Stafford Springs Cemetery is an active historic cemetery whose origins date to 1812. Redesigned in 1883 with elements of the Rural Cemetery movement, its winding roads, mature landscape, ponds, monuments, and historic chapel create a peaceful setting rich in history and character. Traditional burial and cremation lots remain available for families wishing to become part of this historic landscape.

About

Burial & Interment

Traditional burial lots are currently available.

Learn More
Stafford Springs Cemetery continues to provide traditional full-burial interments. Families may contact the Cemetery Association to discuss available burial locations and lot selection. For a full burial, the funeral home generally coordinates the burial date and required disposition paperwork with the Cemetery Association. The Cemetery directs the opening and placement of the grave and records the final interment location. Monuments and markers require cemetery approval and must comply with the cemetery's Rules and Regulations. Please contact the Association before finalizing cemetery arrangements so the burial location and scheduling can be confirmed.

Cremation Options

Cremation burial lots are currently available.

Learn More
Families who already have cremated remains in their care may contact the Stafford Springs Cemetery Association directly to arrange a cremation burial; the cemetery scheduling does not have to be handled through a funeral home. We can assist with available cremation burial locations, lot selection, scheduling, grave placement, marker requirements, and cemetery records. When the burial is scheduled, the Association will advise the family what documentation should accompany the cremated remains. All cremation burials must be scheduled in advance and are subject to the cemetery's Rules and Regulations.

Harwood Memorial Chapel

The Harwood Memorial Chapel is currently closed pending preservation and rehabilitation.

Learn More
Constructed in 1929, the Gothic Revival stone chapel is one of the cemetery's defining historic architectural features. The Association is working toward its preservation and future rehabilitation. The chapel is not currently available for services or public use.
