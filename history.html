History of Stafford Springs Cemetery

Stafford Springs Cemetery reflects more than two centuries of remembrance, community stewardship, landscape design, and preservation. Its grounds tell the story of the families, leaders, and local history that helped shape the area.

Early Origins (1811-1812)

The origins of Stafford Springs Cemetery trace back to 1811, when Miriam Alden selected her own burial site. Upon her passing in 1812, she became the first known burial on the property.

Lieutenant Elisha Alden subsequently set aside the surrounding land for use as a cemetery, establishing the foundation for a permanent place of remembrance.

Expansion and Community Stewardship

As the area developed, members of the community contributed to the expansion and preservation of the cemetery. The Converse family played a significant role by donating land and supporting the long-term care and stewardship of the grounds.

Incorporation (1882)

In 1882, the Stafford Springs Cemetery Association was formally incorporated, creating a structured framework for governance and ongoing care.

Landscape Design (1883)

In 1883, Hartford landscape architect J. Alex McClunie redesigned the cemetery, incorporating elements of the rural cemetery movement, including winding drives and naturalistic scenery.

Civil War Memorial (1897)

The cemetery includes a Civil War memorial featuring a historic Rodman Gun, dedicated in 1897 to honor local contributions to the Union cause.

Early 20th Century Improvements (1912)

Stone entrance features were added in 1912, reinforcing the cemetery's presence while maintaining its historic character.

Harwood Memorial Chapel (1929)

The Harwood Memorial Chapel, constructed in 1929, is a Gothic Revival stone structure gifted by Grace E. Harwood in memory of her parents, Charles F. and Ella Harwood. It remains a defining architectural feature of the cemetery.

The Cemetery Today

Today, Stafford Springs Cemetery continues to serve lot owners while welcoming the public as a place for walking and quiet reflection. The grounds preserve the history of the area, including individuals who contributed to its early development. Through responsible stewardship, preservation, and long-term care, the cemetery remains a lasting and respected part of the community.
